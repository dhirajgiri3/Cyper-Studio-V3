# Creative thesis (Brief 01 v2.1, Step 2) — PROPOSAL, nothing locked

Three one-sentence theses with different stances, each put through three tests: (1) **cover the logo**: could any other SaaS publish this page? (2) **fit the wordmark**: does it sit well with a heavy, wide, flat-blue wordmark? (3) **trust in 30 seconds** for a sceptical logistics founder (Appendix A, 12).

## T1 — the art director's draft (to beat or replace)
> A calm, exact, product-first site where the product is the proof. Everything on the page could only belong to HELIX.

- Cover the logo: **fails the second sentence.** "Calm, exact, product-first" describes most of the good B2B pages in our set (modeinspect, adaline, iru, getenergy). What would make it HELIX-only is not in the sentence. The sentence also depends on a product capture that does not exist yet (Q-13).
- Fit the wordmark: neutral. Nothing in "calm and exact" asks for a heavy wordmark.
- Trust: high intent, but it describes a quality, not a device you can build.

## T2 — wear their name (recommended)
> The page proves white-label by wearing the operator's name: HELIX stays blue and still, and everything the operator's merchants and customers touch changes brand in front of you.

- Cover the logo: **passes.** A page whose own proof object re-brands itself (name, domain, colour) is only possible for a white-label platform. No rented aggregator can publish it truthfully (PRODUCT.md, Positioning).
- Fit the wordmark: **strong.** The heavy blue wordmark is the one fixed element: the engine under every brand. The contrast (blue page chrome, tenant colours inside the window) is the message, and it is why "HELIX blue never appears inside the tenant window" is a design rule and not just a palette note.
- Trust: the claim is shown, not asserted. Risk: it leans on C-06 ("merchants and consumers see only the operator's brand"), which is `[BRIEF-ONLY]` and needs verification on a live tenant (Q-05).
- Builds: HERO-A (the swap), HERO-C (the pair), HERO-D (your own name).

## T3 — an operations document with a heavy letterhead
> A plain operations document with a heavy letterhead: every line is a fact, every screen is labelled, and only the HELIX wordmark is allowed to be loud.

- Cover the logo: **partly passes.** Pages that mark every screen "Sample", separate "live today" from "planned" and carry visible claim tags are rare, but the style is shared by plenty of documentation sites and says nothing about logistics.
- Fit the wordmark: **strong** (the wordmark is the letterhead, the only heavy thing).
- Trust: highest for the sceptic persona (the Vetoer), lowest for conversion: it can read cold.
- Builds: HERO-B (editorial) in spirit; it is also what the truth rules force anyway, so it works better as a constraint on T2 than as a thesis.

## Recommendation
**T2 as the thesis, T3 as its discipline.** T2 gives the page a device only HELIX can own; T3's rules (sample labels, evidence tags, live/planned) are binding regardless (Spec 4). T1's calm and exactness remain the tone, not the idea.

The test T2 has not passed yet: a cold 5-second test with three people outside the team (Appendix A, 1). The founder can run it with the compare page; the protocol is in `PAGE_REVIEW_CHECKLIST.proposed.md`.
